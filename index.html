Hi There!

I am Diego Miura, a 3rd year undergrad at Yale University studying Astrophysics, with Certificates in Data Science and Energy Studies.

I am currently working with the DiRAC Institute at the University of Washington to develop and test Hyrax, an Astro-Machine Learning framework, by looking for anomalous and rare objects in large astronomical datasets.

I'm also a member of the Meg Urry Lab here at Yale, and my research involves doing near-infrared spectroscopy of high-redshift Fermi blazars using Hβ and Mg II λ2800 lines. My previous project (published paper) involved spectroscopy of variable z<1.5 AGN, using their properties such as mass and brightness to study the black hole-host galaxy mass evolution.

Beyond my research, I serve as a Head of Consulting for Elmseed Consulting and as the Events and Communications Chair for the Yale Astronomical Space and Student Society (YASSS).

I also love astrophotography, and the picture below is a stacked image I took at the Leitner Observatory here at Yale!

Research

  • Variable AGN Properties

    Analyzing properties of variability-selected z < 1.5 active galactic nuclei and studying their black hole mass – host mass relation. Paper published to The Astrophysical Journal

  • High z Fermi Blazars

    Near-infrared spectroscopy of high-redshift fermi blazars using Hβ and Mg II λ2800 lines. Project underway.

  • Anomaly Detection using ML

    Developing and testing Hyrax, an astronomical machine learning framework and searching for unusual objects in new data. Project underway.
